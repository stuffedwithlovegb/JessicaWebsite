A DIFFERENT WAY TO CONNECT

Music meets your child
where they are.

There is no one right way to participate.

Music therapy creates opportunities for connection through singing, movement, rhythm, and instrument play. Individual therapy uses these experiences intentionally, with goals tailored to the person.

Your child’s interests, strengths, and needs help shape the experience. There is room to listen, explore, move, and find their own way into the music.

You don’t need to know all the answers before reaching out.

Does my child need musical experience?

No musical skills are required. Music therapy focuses on the person and their goals; participation can take many forms.

How is music therapy different from music lessons?

Music lessons focus on learning musical skills. Individual music therapy uses musical experiences to work toward personalized, non-musical goals. Jessica can also explain how her community classes differ from individual therapy.

What does a session look like?

It may include singing, instruments, movement, listening, and musical play. The activities and pace depend on the individual or group. Ask Jessica what to expect for the service you’re considering.

Where are sessions held?

Contact Jessica to confirm current session locations, service area, and options for your family.

What about pricing and funding?

Jessica can share current rates, payment details, and any funding options that apply to the service you’re interested in.
